From analyst-owned scripts to governed research infrastructure

A research and simulation workflow used by analysts had become person-dependent, slow, and impossible to govern cleanly. We took ownership of standardising it into reusable AWS services, shared libraries, and a client-facing simulation layer that produced consistent, auditable outputs.

The starting point

Analysts and in-house research teams were running backtesting and market research through individually maintained scripts. Each team had its own version. There was no shared standard, no reproducibility across desks, and client requests to simulate a new market opportunity or test an index methodology slowed down whenever the one person who knew the script was unavailable.

The operating move

The first step was not replacing everything at once. We mapped the live landscape, identified who was running what, how often, where the common patterns sat, and which workflows were producing the most friction. Then we focused on the 20% of scripts driving 80% of the use cases and productised those first.

How it was built

Productisation, not script clean-up

The objective was not tidier code. It was a reusable service layer with clean interfaces, shared standards, and consistent outputs teams could trust.

Infrastructure with governance

We worked with the technology team to build the data pipelines, compute layer, and governance controls needed to make the new services stable and auditable.

Research-led standards

Research leads helped define what standard looked like so the platform reflected actual analytical practice instead of top-down theory.

Adoption by better path

Migration was not forced. The new platform became the easier path, with support from research leads and operator-level guidance during the transition.

Result

Research turnaround dropped materially. Client simulation requests that previously required analyst involvement could now be handled directly. Teams stopped duplicating work because they were drawing from a shared, versioned library, and leadership gained visibility for the first time into what research was being run, by whom, and how the results were being used.
