How the work runs

From diagnostic, to first agent, to live in your stack.

Two fixed-scope engagements. The artifact is an agent watching the book, not a deck. We stay on call through the first month of live operation.

§ 01
Diagnostic Two to three weeks

We sit with the team running the book.

We watch where signals are missing, where escalation paths break, where out-of-hours coverage thins, and where the team is acting on incomplete picture.

The output is a prioritised monitoring architecture: which surfaces matter most, what coverage looks like, what signals are needed, what escalation paths should fire, and what the measurable targets are in coverage, signal-to-escalation time, and false positive rate.

§ 02
First agent Six to eight weeks

We pick one surface and build the agent against real data.

News ingestion, error system integration, policy-bound escalation logic, audit trail. We work alongside the team that owns the book, in the operator's stack, not a sandbox.

The agent ships into production with measurable improvement in coverage or response time, and with a named operator on the client side accountable for it.

§ 03
Handover First month live

Policy versioned. Kill switches in place. Evidence ready.

Operator-named ownership on the client side. Policy versioned. Kill switches configured for the head of risk and the ops head. Evidence retention configured to BCBS 239 or the institution's equivalent.

We stay on call through the first month of live operation. After that, the team runs the agent. We do not become a permanent dependency.

What we refuse

The artifact is the agent. Not a deck.

We do not hand back a slide deck. We do not write a strategy paper. We do not run a multi-year programme.

If we leave and the book is not being watched better than when we arrived, the engagement failed.

Who builds the agent

Eight years inside a global investment bank, building LLM analytics platforms, decision systems, and monitoring infrastructure for institutional teams. Direct work across high-value payments, transaction monitoring, and operational risk. Now applying that operating discipline to always-on agents inside regulated financial institutions.
